How to Read RSI Like a Pro: Complete Guide

March 12, 2024 5 min read By Alex Chen

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is one of the most popular and powerful indicators in technical analysis. When used correctly, it can help you identify potential reversals and confirm trends with remarkable accuracy.

What is RSI?

RSI is a momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements. It oscillates between 0 and 100, helping traders identify overbought or oversold conditions.

Key RSI Levels

  • Above 70: Overbought - Potential SELL signal
  • Below 30: Oversold - Potential BUY signal
  • 50-70: Bullish zone - Uptrend
  • 30-50: Bearish zone - Downtrend

RSI Divergence: The Secret Weapon

Divergence occurs when price makes a new high/low but RSI doesn't. This is one of the strongest signals in technical analysis.

Bullish Divergence

Price makes lower low, but RSI makes higher low → Potential reversal UP

Bearish Divergence

Price makes higher high, but RSI makes lower high → Potential reversal DOWN

Candlestick patterns are the foundation of technical analysis. Understanding them can give you a significant edge in your trading.

Single Candle Patterns

Doji

Open and close are almost equal - indicates indecision and potential reversal.

Hammer

Small body with long lower wick - bullish reversal signal after downtrend.

Shooting Star

Small body with long upper wick - bearish reversal signal after uptrend.

Double Candle Patterns

Bullish Engulfing

Red candle followed by larger green candle that engulfs it - strong buy signal.

Bearish Engulfing

Green candle followed by larger red candle that engulfs it - strong sell signal.

Triple Candle Patterns

Morning Star

Long red, small body, long green - powerful bullish reversal.

Evening Star

Long green, small body, long red - powerful bearish reversal.

Risk Management: Never Lose More Than 2%

March 8, 2024 4 min read By Mike Zhang

The difference between successful and unsuccessful traders often comes down to one thing: risk management.

The 2% Rule

Never risk more than 2% of your trading capital on a single trade. This ensures that even a series of losses won't wipe out your account.

Example with $10,000 Account

  • Max risk per trade: $200 (2% of $10,000)
  • If stop loss is $0.50 per share, you can buy 400 shares
  • Position size = Risk amount ÷ Stop loss distance

Position Sizing Calculator

Use this formula: Position Size = (Account Balance × Risk %) / (Entry Price - Stop Loss)

Risk-Reward Ratio

Only take trades with at least 1:2 risk-reward ratio (risk $1 to make $2).
